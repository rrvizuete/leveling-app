1 Overview

This tool turns girder design data into field elevations. It computes the top of each girder, including its dead-load deflection, then applies the same deflection to the theoretical top-of-deck surface. The result is the set of elevations the deck must be built to, so that it reaches its design profile after the girders deflect.

  1. Girder dataFill in the template and upload it on the Girder Calcs tab.
  2. CalculateBuild top-of-girder points and deflection profiles.
  3. Deflected deckUpload the deck DTM, set the overhang, and compute.
  4. ReviewCheck the plan view and sections along an alignment.
  5. ExportDownload points (Excel) and the deflected surface (LandXML).

All work happens in your browser; files are not uploaded to a server.

3 Girder Calcs tab

  1. Choose Download template and fill in one row per girder per span.
  2. Upload the completed sheet under Girder data sheet. The rows appear in an editable grid; you can correct values there before calculating.
  3. Set Intervals (1–250): the number of equal divisions along each girder. Points are produced at every division, so 10 intervals gives 11 stations per girder (00–10).
  4. Choose Calculate, review the Calculation Log, then Export › Top of girder. Exporting is a separate step, so you can recalculate without downloading each time.

4 Graphs tab

The Plan View shows every girder in Northing/Easting. The Deflection Profile shows the selected girder's deflection along its length. Pick a girder with the Span and Girder selectors, or click it in the plan; the two views stay in sync.

View tools

Each tab keeps its inputs in the sidebar on the left, leaving the rest of the screen for the views. Every chart has a row of view tools at its top left:

  • Zoom window (magnifier, on by default): drag a box corner to corner and the view zooms to exactly that box. On the plan views, which keep true proportions, the view widens in one direction to fit the box.
  • Pan and Zoom in / Zoom out.
  • Zoom extents (four arrows) fits everything drawn in the chart. Reset axes (home) returns to the chart's first view.
  • Download (camera) saves the chart as an image.

The plan views keep true proportions and draw a square grid, the same spacing north and east, which refines (for example from 100 ft to 20 ft) as you zoom in.

5 Deflected Deck tab

  1. Run the calculation on the Girder Calcs tab first.
  2. Upload the Top of deck DTM. The status line confirms the point and face count.
  3. Optionally enter an Overhang (ft) and pick the Overhang grade – see Overhang & screed.
  4. Choose Compute deck.

The tool builds a deflection (isopach) surface between the girders and adds it to the deck: deflected elevation = DTM elevation + deflection. Past the span ends the deflection is zero, so the deck keeps its DTM elevation there.

Reading the plan view

  • Plan view surface picks what the colour map shows: the Isopach (deflection; pale is little or none, deep red the most), the Deflected deck elevations, or the Top of deck DTM as imported. The two elevation maps run from dark blue (low) to yellow (high). The DTM can be shown as soon as it is loaded; the other two need Compute deck. Hover anywhere on the deck to read the value.
  • Tick Contours to draw contour lines of the shaded surface at the Interval (ft) beside it (0.1 ft by default). Every fifth contour is heavier and labelled. Contours stop at the deck edge (or the screed line, with an overhang). An interval that would give more than 300 contours is refused with a note under the box.
  • Black is the deck outline, grey the girders. Dashed orange lines are the overhang edges (screed lines).
  • Pick a Span/Girder (or click a girder) to highlight it and label its deflected deck points with their export names. Hover any point for its elevations.

6 Overhang & screed

The deck model usually stops at the edge of deck, but the screed rails sit beyond it. The Overhang extends the deflected deck past the model so elevations can be given where the screed runs.

  • The offset is measured from the edge of deck (where the DTM ends), perpendicular to the deck edge. The deck edge may curve – it usually runs parallel to the alignment – even where the girders are straight, and the screed line follows it.
  • The Overhang grade selector sets how the deck runs across the extension:
    • Carry the cross slope (default) – the deflected deck continues at the deck's cross slope, taken from the last 2 ft of the model, perpendicular to the deck edge.
    • Level from the edge of deck – the overhang is held flat at the deflected edge-of-deck elevation, out to the screed line.
    Changing it after Compute deck recomputes straight away.
  • The edge of deck is a break line. The overhang grade starts exactly there and is never blended back into the deck: the deflected deck follows the DTM (plus deflection) up to the edge, and only the overhang takes the chosen grade. In the surface export no triangle crosses the edge, and the edge is also written as a LandXML break line.
  • The overhang belongs to the deflected deck: it starts at the deflected edge-of-deck elevation, and the exterior girder's deflection is held across the whole overhang, because the overhang cantilevers from it. The OH points and the surface export give deflected elevations.
  • Leave the offset blank for no extension.
Where the numbers go. The overhang edge is exported as OH points at every girder interval, and the deflected surface export (LandXML) runs all the way out to it. Each OH point's Note says whether the deck there was carried at the cross slope (with the slope used) or held level.

7 Alignment sections

  1. On the Deflected Deck tab, upload a Civil alignment (LandXML). Lines, curves, and clothoid spirals are supported.
  2. If the file holds several alignments, choose one from Alignment. The one running closest to the bridge is selected automatically.
  3. Stations across the bridge are listed every Interval (ft). Step through them with the arrows, pick one from the list on the Station box, or type any station in the same box and press Enter (12+34.50 or 1234.50); a typed station joins the list, so the arrows step on from it. You can also click the alignment in the plan view. A station outside the bridge (or, if the bridge is not alongside the alignment, outside the alignment) is flagged under the box and the section stays where it was.

Vert. exag. sets the section's vertical scale relative to its horizontal scale. Left blank, the section fills the view and the box shows the exaggeration in use (e.g. Auto (13)); type that or any other value to hold the same scale from station to station.

Each section is cut square to the alignment and shows:

  • the original deck (blue) and the deflected deck (red), dashed where carried out to the overhang;
  • girders (dotted, labelled G01, G02…), overhang edges (orange, OH), and the alignment centerline (CL).

Offsets are negative to the left of the alignment and positive to the right. The current section line is drawn on the plan view.

Station equations in the alignment file are ignored; stations run continuously from the alignment's start station.

9 Calculation methods

Girder geometry

Each girder runs from Support1 to Support2 along a straight chord, or a circular arc when a radius is given. Position along it is expressed as t, from 0 at Support1 to 1 at Support2. The left and right points are half the girder width either side of the centerline, square to it.

Deflection curve

The deflection is a parabola that is zero at both supports, fitted by least squares to the values given:

d(t) = A · (t² − t) A = Σ vᵢ·bᵢ / Σ bᵢ², bᵢ = tᵢ² − tᵢ t = 1/2 (midspan, required), 1/4 and 1/3 (optional)

Top of girder

Elevation = Seat Z + Bearing + Plate + Girder height + d(t)

Seat Z, bearing, and plate heights are interpolated linearly between the supports. Camber is reported but not added.

Deflected deck

Girder deflections are joined into a triangulated surface between neighbouring girders at matching intervals (the isopach). The deck is then

Deflected Z = DTM Z + deflection(N, E)

The overhang holds the exterior girder's deflection. Beyond the span ends the deflection is zero.

Overhang extension

Deflected Z(screed) = Deflected Z(edge of deck) + s · offset Deflected Z(edge of deck) = DTM Z(edge of deck) + exterior girder deflection s = cross slope over the last 2 ft of the deck model, perpendicular to the deck edge (Carry the cross slope) s = 0 (Level from the edge of deck)

The overhang is part of the deflected deck: it starts at the deflected edge-of-deck elevation and keeps the exterior girder's deflection. The edge of deck is a break line, so the grade changes from the deck's to the overhang's at that line only.

Deflected surface export

Deck DTM triangles can be tens of feet long, while the deflection curves along the span. To capture it, each DTM triangle is cut on a 5 ft plan grid. Every piece keeps its DTM triangle's plane, so crowns and breaklines are unchanged, and the deflection is added at every vertex. A strip is then added along both deck edges out to the overhang offset, at the chosen overhang grade. The strip is hung from the deck's own edge vertices, so the edge of deck stays a break line (no triangle crosses it); it is also written to the file as <Breakline> polylines, so software that rebuilds the TIN keeps the break. The result is a single, crack-free TIN.

10 QC & troubleshooting

Check the Calculation Log after each step. Common messages:

Message or symptomWhat it means / what to do
DTM WARNING: surface only overlaps the girders when N and E are swapped The DTM was exported as E N Z instead of N E Z. Re-export it, or check the exporter settings.
QC WARNING: the entire DTM sits below the top of girder The file is probably not the finished top of deck, or it uses a different vertical datum or units.
Deflected deck ERROR: DTM extents do not overlap the girder extents The DTM and girder sheet are in different coordinate systems.
Isopach WARNING: needs at least two girders A span has a single girder; deflection is treated as zero across it.
Overhang WARNING: the DTM does not cover this exterior girder The edge of deck could not be found, so the offset is measured from the girder centerline instead.
The bridge is not alongside this alignment The chosen alignment ends at or crosses the bridge. Choose one that runs along the deck.
Station … does not cross the deck or any girder The station is off the bridge; pick one from the list.
New buttons or inputs are missing after an update Restart the server and reload the page with Ctrl+F5.
